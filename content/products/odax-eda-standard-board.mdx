## Overview

The ODAX EDA Standard Board (SB1) is an assembled analog sensor board for electrodermal activity (EDA) and galvanic skin response (GSR) experiments.

It uses the same circuit and electrical routing as the [ODAX EDA Maker board](/products/odax-eda-maker), part number `ODAX-EDA-MB1-100-ASM-R1`, in a different mechanical form factor. The Standard Board measures **35 × 31 mm** and provides **four corner mounting holes** and **three through-hole solder connections** for integration into enclosures, test fixtures, and custom builds.

The board applies a low-voltage electrode bias, measures the resulting skin-current response with a transimpedance amplifier (TIA) analog front end, and provides a ground-referenced analog output for a microcontroller ADC. Higher skin conductance produces higher `VOUT`.

It is intended for relative EDA/GSR trend measurement, event detection, biofeedback experiments, and custom firmware development. It is not a medical device and is not intended for diagnosis, monitoring, treatment, or safety-critical use.

## Why Choose the Standard Board

- The same electrical behavior as the ODAX EDA Maker board in an alternative PCB shape
- Four mounting holes for securing the board inside an enclosure or fixture
- Through-hole solder connections for suitable wires or compatible pins
- A simple analog output for your own sampling, logging, filtering, and event-detection firmware
- Compatibility with two-electrode lead setups and ODAX EDA contact straps

## Key Features

- Maker-grade TIA-based analog EDA/GSR front end
- Positive-slope, ground-referenced output for a high-impedance ADC input
- Recommended 3 V to 5 V battery-powered operation while electrodes contact skin
- Approximately DC to 6 Hz useful signal bandwidth for slow EDA/GSR signals
- 3.5 mm TS electrode interface for two skin-contact electrodes
- 35 × 31 mm PCB with a four-hole mounting pattern

## Applications

- EDA and GSR measurement for prototyping and maker projects
- Psychophysiology, HCI, affective computing, biometrics, and biofeedback experiments
- Wearable sensing prototypes using standard electrode lead sets
- Custom enclosures and acquisition systems for low-frequency analog biosignals

### User Connections

The board labels `PWR` and `OUT` correspond to `VIN` and `VOUT` in the Maker board documentation. The electrical functions are unchanged.

| Connection | Function | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `PWR` | `VIN`; input | DC supply input. Recommended 3 V to 5 V from a battery-powered system while electrodes contact skin |
| `GND` | Reference | Common ground for the board and the ADC system |
| `OUT` | `VOUT`; output | Analog EDA/GSR output. Connect to a high-impedance ADC input |
| 3.5 mm jack tip | `E+` | Electrode drive contact. Connect to one skin-contact electrode or strap |
| 3.5 mm jack sleeve | `E-` | Measurement return contact. Connect to the second skin-contact electrode or strap |
| 3.5 mm jack ring | `NC` | If a TRS jack or plug is used mechanically, the ring contact is not used by the board |

With the component side facing you and the electrode jack at the bottom, the upper solder connections are **PWR, GND, OUT from left to right**. Verify the labels on the supplied board before wiring. Do not intentionally connect either electrode contact to `GND`.

The board uses through-hole solder connections without a fitted terminal block. Solder suitable wires or compatible pins as required, taking care not to damage the PCB pads or nearby components.

## Getting Started

1. With power off, solder suitable wires or compatible pins to `PWR`, `GND`, and `OUT`, checking the board labels before wiring.
2. Connect `OUT` to a high-impedance ADC input and share common `GND` with the ADC system.
3. Attach two skin-contact electrodes or ODAX EDA contact straps through the 3.5 mm electrode jack.
4. Use a battery-powered 3 V to 5 V supply connected to `PWR` and `GND`.
5. Place the contacts on adjacent fingers of the same hand, keep pressure comfortable and consistent, and route the cable to minimize motion.

Use battery power only while electrodes contact skin. Do not power the board from a mains adapter while connected to a person, or connect it to mains-referenced instruments unless the complete system has appropriate isolation designed and verified by a qualified person. Do not exceed 5.5 V on `PWR` or drive `OUT` from another source.

End-system performance depends on electrode condition, skin condition, contact pressure, motion, firmware filtering, ADC resolution, and user calibration.
